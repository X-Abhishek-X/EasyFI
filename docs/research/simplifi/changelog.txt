What’s new

Changes you can see in SimpliFi. Newest release first.

0.5.0October 17, 2026

Every portfolio has a Holdings page with a full holdings table, and the Portfolios page opens with your combined value, returns, income, and allocation.

Added
Each portfolio has a Holdings page (Portfolios › name › Holdings) that replaces the Manage holdings window. Add, edit, and delete holdings, edit cash, and connect accounts on one page.
Roth 401(k) is its own account type, next to 401(k) and Roth IRA, when you import a brokerage or add a holding.
The holdings table has Overview, Returns, Income, and Look-through views. Sort by any column, choose which columns show, see totals, select several holdings to delete or tag at once, and export to CSV.
Look-through columns show your true weight in each company, direct plus what you own through funds, and how many of your funds hold it.
Income columns show each holding’s projected annual dividends, yield, yield on cost, and the last 12 months paid.
The allocation chart on a portfolio, Home, and the Portfolios page opens on Portfolio: each fund and stock you hold, with its weight and day change. Click a holding to see its value and weight in each portfolio. Sector, Assets, and Holding stay look-through.
Help, in the footer, collects short answers to questions we hear. If yours is not there, email us from Contact.
Help explains that cancelling a subscription keeps your login and portfolios, and that permanently deleting the account is a separate email request.
Help covers Canadian brokerages and Toronto-listed ETFs: Questrade and Wealthsimple connect, US-dollar holdings import, and company look-through stays on US-listed funds.
Help also covers linked brokerage accounts, where income totals are, what the total tracked value chart measures, and the purchasing-power calculator for a custom amount.
Settings, on the card titled Linked brokerages, and a portfolio's Holdings page both link to Choose which accounts go in a portfolio, so an already connected account can move without logging in at the bank again.
Fixed
Portfolio cash in Stack and Constellation shows as Cash, not as ticker $CASH labeled Stock. Linked brokerage cash is unchanged.
Linking a brokerage with many accounts (for example a Fidelity household) no longer fails with a raw error. You can import up to 40 accounts at a time; if you have more, uncheck some and import the rest after. A missing subscription now says so instead of showing computer code.
A brokerage account that has not sent balances yet says Waiting for brokerage data, instead of a blank value, so you can preview again after it fills in.
Dialogs stay centered on iPad Safari when the address bar or keyboard resizes the screen.
Add holding search includes US ETFs such as IZRL when they are not in the curated list. Adding the same stock again keeps the account you picked, and the confirmation names it. Signing out and back in returns you to the page you were on.
Payout frequency for a fund that pays income and a capital gain a few weeks apart once a year now says Annual. Projected income uses that annual pace.
Home and portfolio pages no longer sometimes show an error when every section loads at once, and a holding we cannot find a price for no longer makes the page wait.
Improved
The Portfolios page opens with value, cost basis, unrealized gain, day change, and passive income across your portfolios, plus a value chart and allocation, in place of the four summary cards.
Home, the Portfolios page, and a portfolio overview can expand the value chart or the allocation chart. The larger value chart shows the period’s start, high, and low. Allocation names more of the donut and lists every holding, sector, or asset class, with search when the list is long.
The Portfolios page Holdings tab replaces All positions. A fund held in several portfolios is one row you can expand to see each portfolio’s line.
Adding a holding opens in a side panel, with Add and add another for entering several in a row.
Spot crypto such as BTC-USD has its own Crypto filter instead of appearing under Stock.
When a linked brokerage needs to be reconnected, the portfolio shows a banner with a Reconnect button, and it is marked on the Portfolios page and Home. Settings has Reconnect too. Unlink is a labelled button so it is not mistaken for Reconnect. Linked accounts now say when they were last updated.
0.4.0October 11, 2026

Portfolio value now includes every position your brokerage reports, share-class tickers like Berkshire Hathaway B resolve to one name, and look-through covers far more funds.

Fixed
Treasuries, CDs, bonds, options, and other positions your brokerage values now come in when you link an account and count in your portfolio value at the brokerage’s own value. Lines that cannot be checked against your account total are held back and retried on the next sync.
Berkshire Hathaway B (and other share classes such as Brown-Forman and Lennar) resolve to one ticker everywhere, so funds that hold it are found no matter how the fund spells it.
Linked accounts sync every day again. A late brokerage update no longer causes a missed sync, and a daily check catches any account that falls behind.
When your brokerage disables a connection and stops sharing updates, SimpliFi now notices within a day and shows “needs reconnect” instead of data that no longer updates.
An unavailable look-through now says why, such as a fund that publishes no holdings file.
Improved
Look-through works for ETFs and mutual funds outside our curated list: we find their holdings from the SEC filing the first time you view or hold one.
Gold and silver trusts (GLD, IAU, SLV and similar) show their metal as the holding, and the Amplify ETF HACK shows its full holdings.
You can link more than one account of the same type, such as two taxable accounts, into one portfolio. Duplicate positions show which account they belong to.
A stock page shows “held by N of the M funds we track”, and each linked account shows the date of its brokerage data.
Added
Linked-account preview shows your brokerage’s total next to SimpliFi’s, and lists anything not counted yet.
In Manage holdings, add a Treasury, CD, option, or other brokerage-valued position by hand with its current value.
0.3.0October 3, 2026

Holdings can no longer be saved without a value, 401(k) lines with no public price have a proper place, small positions show accurately, and allocation reads as stocks, bonds, and cash.

Fixed
In Manage holdings, the pencil scrolls to the edit form and places the cursor in shares, or in current value for a statement line.
A direct position smaller than 0.1% of the portfolio shows as “<0.1%” instead of 0.0%, so it no longer reads as not held.
A stock you hold directly in more than one account, such as taxable and a Roth IRA, is added together in look-through.
A direct position with no price is labeled Unpriced instead of $0.00.
Money market funds, including ones whose tickers end in XX, count as cash instead of a stock holding.
A stock you hold directly shows its company name, including in the allocation legend. A money market fund you hold directly shows its fund name.
Company names use one Inc. style, keep letters like S&P and AT&T, and drop the SEC /DE/ suffix.
Large companies held through international funds, such as Taiwan Semiconductor, Samsung, Tencent, Alibaba, ASML, and Nestlé, are matched to their US listing and classified. Streamex, Unilever’s older UN ticker, and the Japanese trading-company ADRs are classified too.
Sector and industry for a stock you add are looked up from the full SEC company list, not only from funds already in the app. Loans and funds held inside other funds stay in the right bucket.
An international stock that is not yet classified by industry still counts as Stocks in the Assets view, not Other.
Companies whose ticker matches a coin symbol, such as Sasol or Link REIT, are no longer counted as crypto.
A tiny crypto line no longer shows up as a 0.0% top holding.
A name that appears in several funds is noted as overlap. That note no longer turns a small position orange.
Fixed Income, cash, and crypto are not reported as the top industry.
A tag total leaves out positions with no cost basis instead of counting them as $0.
Treasury notes inside a fund, such as an inflation-protected bond fund, count as Bonds. A currency balance inside a fund counts as cash. A fund held inside another fund counts as a fund even when its name does not say ETF.
Baidu is classified as Communication Services. Companies filed under a catch-all SEC code get a more specific sector when one is available.
A company abroad whose ticker matches a US ETF or trust, such as Schindler (SCHP) or Barco (BAR), is classified as that company, not as a fund or a commodity broker.
Improved
Adding a holding by shares or dollars needs a price. When a ticker has no price, Manage holdings says so, asks you to check the ticker, and keeps Add holding off.
Shares, dollars, cost basis, statement value, and cash accept a thousands comma and a leading dollar sign, so a value copied from a statement such as 23,242.82 or $1,234.50 can be pasted in. A comma that does not separate thousands, such as 1,23, is still rejected.
Ticker search says “Not found. Check the ticker” when nothing matches and there is no price, instead of offering to add it anyway.
Stack details and the constellation panel show the dollar amount of each slice next to its weight.
A holding saved earlier without a price can be fixed from Edit by entering its statement value. You can still change its account, cost basis, or tags. Changing its share count needs a price.
Portfolio value and total tracked value captions say day change, week change, and month change instead of today or past week, so a weekend view is not labeled today.
Added
No public price? Enter statement value. Say what the holding is (collective trust, stable value fund, company stock fund, other fund, or non-US or other), then enter the value and date from your statement. It is used for allocation as of that date and left out of today’s change.
The holdings list shows those lines as Statement with the kind, for example “Statement · Stable value fund.” They appear under the Mutual fund filter, except non-US or other.
Allocation has an Assets view: Stocks, Bonds, Cash, Commodities, and Crypto. Bond funds are not listed as an industry.
The allocation card names the portfolio, so a screenshot still says which book it is.
In a sector’s other slice, each category opens. A weight opens where you hold that name.
All positions can be filtered by tag, with market value, cost, and gain for that tag.
Add holding accepts a CUSIP or ISIN and looks up the ticker.
Connect a brokerage as a read-only link. You agree to SnapTrade’s terms before the portal opens. Positions you import refresh on SnapTrade’s daily schedule; unlink keeps them as manual holdings.
The privacy policy names Amplitude for product analytics, including Explorer conversations. Session recordings mask on-screen text, and form entries are not sent. Contact and payment details in a conversation are redacted.
0.2.0September 26, 2026

Portfolio analytics match the holdings more closely, portfolios are easier to scan and edit, and dividend income is easier to read.

Fixed
In Constellation, “more holdings” is how many of that fund’s holdings were folded into the collapsed tail. A fund whose names are already on screen has no collapsed row.
A portfolio of only individual stocks lists those positions in Stack. Overlap appears once an ETF is added.
Cash, money-market funds, and funds that were not looked through are not marked concentrated.
On the allocation chart, a position appears once. Funds are labeled Fund and cash is labeled Cash.
Berkshire Hathaway stays in insurance, including the ticker spellings that show up inside fund holdings.
Fidelity money-market funds FZDXX and FETXX are treated as cash, along with SPAXX.
Improved
The tickers under each portfolio name are ordered by how much that position is worth, largest first. A position with no price stays at the end.
The top look-through holding shows the company and its weight, with a second line for how you hold it: Direct, through one or more ETFs, or both.
An operating company is labeled concentrated when it is at least 5% of the portfolio. A small name that appears in several ETFs stays a lighter overlap label. The label describes your holdings. It is not a forecast or a trade.
When a meaningful share of the portfolio could not be expanded, diversification and concentration scores leave that slice out, and the exposure views name the funds that were not looked through.
Edit a portfolio’s name and type (Actual, Hypothetical, or Watchlist) from the portfolios list, or from the pencil next to the name on the portfolio page.
Settings and Account stay at the bottom of the sidebar while the page scrolls. Account is where you sign out.
Income opens on all portfolios and remembers the last portfolio type you chose.
Help tips open when you tap them.
On a phone, charts scroll with the page. Wide tables show Swipe while more columns sit off-screen. Range controls, lens tabs, allocation, Stack, and the constellation stay on the screen.
The daily mover constellation names the largest holdings and collapses the rest into one bubble. That bubble links to the fund page.
A portfolio card on Home opens that portfolio. The ticker links on the card still open the holding.
The daily mover on Home prefers a fund you hold.
Ticker colors on a Home portfolio card are easier to tell apart.
Sector and industry lists start with the larger weights. Show all opens the rest.
Company names are tidied when the source text is noisy, such as “Amazon COM Inc.”
The dividend calendar lists each payment as a date, ticker, and amount.
Dividend holdings show the fund or company name, and payout frequency is filled in from how often the cash arrives. A monthly payer that also pays extras is labeled Monthly + specials.
The income chart keeps later months after the next declared payment. Click any month, including months already received. The current month includes cash already paid and what is still ahead.
Under the income chart, this calendar year shows what you have received, what is still projected, and an estimated total.
The dividend holdings table includes a % of income column. Filter chips are All, ETFs, Funds, and Stocks.
A holding with a short dividend history shows New instead of a very large growth percent.
Income concentration treats a diversified fund more loosely than a single stock. Money-market income is left out of that check. A high-yield stock can still be flagged. The headline states the reason once.
The change under the top dividend cards reads “vs a year ago.” The help tip explains trailing 12 months: the last year of distributions on your current shares, compared with the same stretch a year earlier.
Dividend totals can include capital-gains distributions when the feed does not separate them from dividends. The holdings table says so.
A money-market fund such as SPAXX opens a fund page labeled Cash equivalent, instead of a stock page with no holdings.
Other mutual funds open a fund page. Holdings come from the fund’s SEC report, which is quarterly and published about 60 days later.
Filter holdings by ETF, mutual fund, stock, or cash. Bonds appear when the portfolio has them. Mutual funds are listed on their own, not under ETF.
In Manage holdings, sort the list by ticker, shares, value, average cost, unrealized gain, account, or source. Text columns start A to Z. Number columns start with the highest value. A position with no price stays at the end, and cash stays at the bottom.
Search that list by ticker, tag, account, or source. Search works together with the asset-type and tag filters.
Tag filters sit apart from the All, ETF, and Stock buttons, and the tag under a ticker reads as a label.
Added
IonQ, Rigetti, Quantum Computing Inc., and D-Wave are classified as Quantum Computing. Their SEC filings still list them under IT consulting, application software, or data processing. SimpliFi shows the industry that matches what these companies do.
Fidelity Contrafund (FCNTX) is looked through to the companies it holds.
Fidelity index mutual funds are looked through with an equivalent ETF book. FXAIX (500 Index) and FNILX (ZERO Large Cap) use IVV. FSKAX (Total Market) and FZROX (ZERO Total Market) use ITOT. FSMDX (Mid Cap) uses IJH. FSSNX (Small Cap) uses IWM. FTIHX (Total International) and FZILX (ZERO International) use IXUS. FXNAX (U.S. Bond) uses AGG. FZIPX (ZERO Extended Market) uses VXF. Your shares stay on the mutual-fund ticker.
Tags on a holding, such as growth or value. Add them when you add or edit a holding, then filter the holdings list by tag.
Feedback in the footer, for a bug, a feature idea, or a general note. A screenshot is optional.
